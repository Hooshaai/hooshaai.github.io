Mathematical Intuition: Continuous Flow Matching vs Stochastic Diffusion

1. Linear Flow Trajectories & Vector Fields

Flow Matching replaces noisy simulation paths with linear interpolation between source noise $x_0 \sim p_0$ and target data $x_1 \sim q$:

$$x_t = (1 - t) x_0 + t x_1 \implies v_t(x_t) = \frac{d x_t}{d t} = x_1 - x_0$$

The target velocity vector field $v_t(x_t)$ is constant along each linear sample trajectory $x_0 \to x_1$.

2. Simulation-Free Loss Objective

Instead of integrating expensive SDE paths during training, CFM trains a neural network $v_\theta(x_t, t)$ using regression:

$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{CFM}}(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{t, q(x_0, x_1)} \left\| v_\theta(x_t, t) - (x_1 - x_0) \right\|^2$$

Optimal Transport (OT) alignment minimizes total displacement energy $\int_0^1 \|v_t\|^2 dt$.

3. Why Diffusion SDEs Are Slower

Traditional VP-Diffusion relies on Brownian motion stochastic differential equations:

$$d x_t = -\frac{1}{2}\beta(t) x_t d t + \sqrt{\beta(t)} d W_t$$

Stochastic noise $dW_t$ introduces curved trajectories, requiring 50–100 ODE steps to avoid discretization drift. CFM achieves straight ODE paths in 4–10 steps.

Mathematical Intuition: Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)

1. Group Advantage Normalization

Instead of estimating expected value $V(s)$ with a Critic neural network, GRPO calculates advantages $A_i$ directly from $G$ sampled outputs for prompt $q$:

$$A_i = \frac{r_i - \text{mean}(\{r_1, \dots, r_G\})}{\text{std}(\{r_1, \dots, r_G\}) + \delta}$$

Outputs scoring above the group baseline get positive advantage ($A_i > 0$), while below-average outputs get penalized ($A_i < 0$).

2. Clipped Surrogate Objective

The policy $\pi_\theta$ is updated via PPO-style clipped surrogate objective across all candidates in the group:

$$\mathcal{J}_{\text{CLIP}}(\theta) = \frac{1}{G} \sum_{i=1}^G \min\left( \frac{\pi_\theta(o_i|q)}{\pi_{\text{old}}(o_i|q)} A_i, \text{clip}\left(\frac{\pi_\theta(o_i|q)}{\pi_{\text{old}}(o_i|q)}, 1-\epsilon, 1+\epsilon\right) A_i \right)$$

Clipping ratio bounds prevent destabilizingly large parameter steps during policy gradient updates.

3. Reference Model KL Regularization

To keep reasoning generations coherent and prevent policy collapse, a per-token KL divergence penalty is applied:

$$\mathcal{J}_{\text{GRPO}}(\theta) = \mathcal{J}_{\text{CLIP}}(\theta) - \beta D_{\text{KL}}(\pi_\theta || \pi_{\text{ref}})$$

Mathematical Intuition: Differential Attention Architecture

1. Scaled Attention Subtraction

Differential Attention projects input features $X$ into two separate query-key head pairs $(Q_1, K_1)$ and $(Q_2, K_2)$ and subtracts their softmax maps:

$$\text{DiffAttn}(X) = \left( \text{softmax}\left(\frac{Q_1 K_1^T}{\sqrt{d}}\right) - \lambda \cdot \text{softmax}\left(\frac{Q_2 K_2^T}{\sqrt{d}}\right) \right) V$$

Map $A_1$ captures signal + noise, while map $A_2$ acts as a noise estimator. Subtraction cancels background attention activation.

2. Spatial High-Pass Noise Filter

In standard softmax $\text{softmax}(QK^T / \sqrt{d})$, probability mass spreads across thousands of irrelevant background tokens in long context windows:

$$\lim_{N \to \infty} \sum_{i \in \text{noise}} \text{softmax}(Q K_i^T / \sqrt{d}) \approx 1 - \epsilon_{\text{signal}}$$

3. Attention Entropy Minimization

The concentration of attention on true signal tokens is quantified by Attention Entropy $H(A)$:

$$H(A) = -\sum_{j=1}^N A_j \log_2 A_j$$
